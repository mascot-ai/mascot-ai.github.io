> **TL;DR**
>
> - A good topic is specific, personal, and something only you could write. Use these 50 ideas as prompts to think with. Do not copy them as topics.
> - Start from a moment or object, not from a big theme. The big theme comes out of the writing.
> - Run any idea through three tests before you commit: Can I picture a scene? Do I have something to say about it? Does it show something the rest of my application does not?

Most "topic idea" lists hand you a category, like "write about overcoming a challenge." The ideas below are smaller. Each one points at a kind of material, and your job is to find the specific version that is true for you.

If you want the thinking behind this list first, read [What makes a college essay topic actually work](/blog/what-makes-a-college-essay-topic-work/) and [How to figure out what to write your college essay about](/blog/how-to-choose-college-essay-topic/).

## Everyday moments

1. A task you do so often you no longer notice it.
2. A family routine that would confuse an outsider.
3. A meal you make, or one you will not eat.
4. The commute or walk you know by heart.
5. A thing you fix, again and again, instead of replacing.
6. A job you have had, even a small one, and what it taught you about people.
7. A place you go to be alone.
8. A conversation that changed how you saw something small.

There is more on why small moments often beat dramatic ones in [How to turn an ordinary moment into a strong essay topic](/blog/ordinary-moment-college-essay/).

## Objects and places

9. An object you keep for reasons that make no sense to anyone else.
10. A room in your home and what it says about the people who live there.
11. A map, a street, or a neighborhood that changed while you watched.
12. A tool, instrument, or piece of equipment you know better than anyone.
13. Something you collect.
14. A place you were afraid to go, and went.
15. A piece of clothing with a story.

## Hobbies and obsessions

16. A topic you could talk about for an hour with no notes.
17. A skill you taught yourself from videos or trial and error.
18. The thing you spend time on that you would never put on a resume.
19. A game, sport, or craft where you found a strategy no one taught you.
20. Something you were bad at for a long time.
21. A question you keep returning to and have not answered.

## Disagreements and changed minds

22. A belief you used to hold and no longer do.
23. A time you argued with someone you respect.
24. A rule you broke for a good reason, or followed for a bad one.
25. A moment you realized you were wrong.
26. A tradition you question, and why you still keep it.
27. A time you stayed quiet and wish you had not, or the reverse.

## People

28. A relative whose way of seeing things you picked up.
29. A teacher, coach, or boss who treated you differently than you expected.
30. A friendship that ended or changed.
31. Someone you looked after.
32. A stranger who left a mark.

## Responsibility and work

33. Something you took on because no one else would.
34. A role in your family that others do not see.
35. A project that failed, and what you did next.
36. A time you led without a title.
37. A system you built to solve a problem nobody asked you to solve.

## Identity and background

38. A word, phrase, or name that carries more than its meaning.
39. Moving between two languages, cultures, or communities.
40. A place you come from, described in a way that surprises people who live there.
41. A stereotype you are tired of, and the specific evidence against it.
42. The way your family talks about money, work, or success.

## Learning and curiosity

43. A class where you did more than you were asked.
44. Something you taught yourself because school did not cover it.
45. A time you asked a question that made a room go quiet.
46. A book, film, or song that rearranged your thinking, and the specific line or scene that did it.
47. A problem in your community you cannot stop noticing.

## Looking forward

48. What you would build, fix, or study if there were no grades.
49. A question you want a college to help you answer.
50. A small thing you want to do every day for the rest of your life.

## Three tests before you commit

Pick two or three ideas that pull at you and run each through these:

1. Can you picture a scene? If you can describe one specific moment, with where you were and what happened, you have something to write. If you can only summarize, keep looking.
2. Do you have something to say about it? An event is not an essay. The essay is what you understood, or noticed, or changed your mind about.
3. Does it add something new? Your activity list, grades, and recommendations already cover a lot. The best essay shows a side of you they do not.

Then check the idea against the [topics admissions readers see every cycle](/blog/college-essay-topics-to-avoid/) and the [Common App prompts](/blog/common-app-essay-prompts-explained/). Almost any honest topic fits at least one prompt.

## Not sure which idea is yours?

Reading a list is a weak way to find a story. Saying things out loud works better, because you hear which ones you keep coming back to. [Mascot](/features/) works that way: you talk through a topic, it asks follow-up questions, and you find out which of your stories have enough in them.
